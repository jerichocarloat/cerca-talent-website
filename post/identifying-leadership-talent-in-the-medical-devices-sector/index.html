How to find the right medical device leader

By Scott RiversOct 17, 20246 min read

Originally published as: Identifying Leadership Talent in the Medical Devices Sector

At a glance

Look for leaders who understand your technology, the rules for your products and the markets you serve. Explain the work they will lead and ask how they have handled similar challenges. A focused search and a clear reason to join can help you reach people who are not actively looking for a job.

The medical devices industry sits at the cutting edge of healthcare innovation, merging advanced technology with medical expertise to enhance patient outcomes worldwide.

As the sector experiences unprecedented growth and transformation, the need for visionary leadership becomes increasingly critical. Identifying and securing top-tier leadership talent is not just about filling positions, it's about steering companies through a complex landscape of technological advancements, regulatory hurdles, and global market dynamics.

Challenges in Identifying Leadership Talent

Medical device leaders need to understand the technology and the clinical problem it addresses. For example, Medtronic's MiniMed 780G system uses glucose sensor readings to adjust insulin delivery automatically. Ask leadership candidates how they have worked with technical and clinical teams, assessed evidence and guided product development.

Medical device rules differ across markets. The Food and Drug Administration regulates devices in the United States. In the European Union, the Medical Device Regulation has applied since 26 May 2021, and designated organizations called notified bodies assess many devices before they receive a CE mark. The European Medicines Agency has specific roles for certain device categories. Look for leaders with regulatory experience relevant to your products and target markets.

Globalization presents another layer of complexity. As companies expand into emerging markets such as China, India, and Brazil, they encounter diverse regulatory environments, cultural differences, and varying market needs. For instance, a device approved in the U.S. might require additional clinical trials to meet China's regulatory standards. Leaders must be adept at international business strategies and possess cultural sensitivity to navigate these waters effectively. The shortage of executives with extensive global experience makes the search for suitable leadership even more challenging.

Medical device companies compete with other employers for leaders who can work across science and technology. A job advert alone may not reach suitable people who are already employed. Make the opportunity clear: explain the product challenge, the team's resources, the leader's authority and how success will be judged.

Moreover, the need for specialized skill sets cannot be overstated. Leaders must blend clinical expertise with business acumen and technological proficiency. Take, for example, the CEO of a company developing robotic surgical systems. They must understand the intricacies of surgical procedures, robotics engineering, and market commercialization strategies. Such multifaceted expertise is rare, and identifying individuals who embody this combination requires a strategic and resource-intensive search.

Trends in the Medical Devices Sector

Understanding industry trends is crucial in shaping the criteria for leadership roles.

Digital transformation is revolutionizing healthcare delivery. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated the adoption of telemedicine and remote patient monitoring. Companies like Philips have developed tele-ICU solutions, allowing specialists to monitor patients remotely. Leaders must embrace digital health trends, ensuring their companies remain competitive and relevant.

Some medical devices can be made to match an individual patient's anatomy. The Food and Drug Administration describes 3D-printed implants, surgical instruments and other patient-matched devices. Leaders working in this area need to understand the design, manufacturing and evidence requirements for their products. Claims about better outcomes or shorter recovery times need evidence for the particular device.

Philips completed its BioTelemetry acquisition on 9 February 2021. The deal added remote cardiac diagnostics and monitoring services. Leaders handling acquisitions need to plan how the teams, operations and services will work together.

A growing focus on patient-centric care emphasizes the importance of user-friendly devices that enhance patient engagement and adherence. Companies are investing in human-centered design to improve device usability. Dexcom's continuous glucose monitoring system, which offers real-time data via a smartphone app, exemplifies this trend. Leaders must prioritize patient experience in their strategies.

Strategies for Identifying and Attracting Leadership Talent

Addressing the talent challenge requires a multifaceted approach.

Partnering with specialized executive search firms is an effective strategy. These firms have deep industry knowledge and networks that can uncover hidden talent pools. For instance, a medical device company struggling to find a leader with experience in both AI and regulatory compliance might leverage an executive search firm to identify a candidate who successfully led an AI-based medical device through FDA approval.

Enhancing employer branding is also crucial. Companies must position themselves as innovators and thought leaders in the industry. Showcasing a commitment to cutting-edge research, employee development, and social responsibility can attract leaders who are passionate about making a significant impact. Johnson & Johnson, for example, is known for its strong emphasis on ethics and patient care, which appeals to mission-driven executives.

Competitive compensation packages are essential but should go beyond salary. Offering equity, performance incentives, and opportunities for professional growth can make a position more attractive. For example, a company might offer a leadership role with the autonomy to build a new division or lead international expansion efforts.

Cultivating a culture of innovation can draw visionary leaders. Companies like Stryker encourage intrapreneurship, allowing employees to develop and pitch new ideas. This environment can attract leaders who value creativity and want to drive meaningful change within the organization.

Leveraging professional networks and industry events can also yield results. Active participation in conferences, webinars, and industry associations increases visibility. For instance, presenting at the Medical Device & Manufacturing (MD&M) conference can position a company as a thought leader and attract potential candidates who are engaged in industry advancements.

Conclusion

Identifying leadership talent in the medical devices sector is a complex endeavor, influenced by rapid technological changes, stringent regulations, and global market shifts. Companies need leaders who are not only proficient in navigating these challenges but also possess the vision to drive innovation and growth.

By employing strategic approaches such as partnering with specialized executive search firms, enhancing employer branding, and fostering a culture of innovation, companies can improve their chances of attracting and retaining top-tier leadership talent.
