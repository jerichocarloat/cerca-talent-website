Plan the people side of a health technology merger

By Scott RiversSep 23, 20248 min read

Originally published as: The Crucial Role of Recruitment in Health Tech Mergers and Acquisitions

At a glance

When health technology companies merge or buy another business, their teams and systems need to work together. Plan for the skills, responsibilities and working relationships the combined company will need. Hiring people who understand both healthcare and technology can support that work, alongside careful planning for regulations and data.

The healthcare landscape is undergoing a transformative shift, driven by rapid advancements in technology. Among the most significant trends fueling this evolution is the surge in mergers and acquisitions (M&A) within the health tech sector.

These strategic moves are not merely about consolidation but are pivotal in integrating cutting-edge digital technologies to enhance patient care, streamline operations, and foster innovation. This blog post delves into the dynamics of M&A in health tech, explores the motivations behind these deals, and highlights real-world examples that underscore this trend. Additionally, we’ll examine how recruitment, facilitated by specialized agencies like Cerca Talent, is evolving to meet the demands of a hybrid expertise in both health and technology.

The Rising Tide of M&A in Health Tech

In recent years, the health tech industry has witnessed an unprecedented wave of mergers and acquisitions. This surge is propelled by several factors, including the need for healthcare providers to adopt digital solutions rapidly, the desire to expand service offerings, and the imperative to remain competitive in a landscape increasingly dominated by technological innovation.

Integration of Digital Technologies

One of the primary drivers of M&A activity in health tech is the integration of digital technologies aimed at improving patient outcomes. From telemedicine platforms to electronic health records (EHR) systems and artificial intelligence (AI)-powered diagnostic tools, companies are seeking to acquire or merge with firms that can provide these essential technologies. By doing so, they can offer more comprehensive and seamless services to patients, enhancing the overall healthcare experience.

Enhancing Patient Care

At the core of these mergers and acquisitions is the objective to elevate patient care. Health tech companies are pooling resources and expertise to develop solutions that are not only innovative but also scalable and sustainable. This includes advancements in personalized medicine, remote patient monitoring, and data analytics, all of which contribute to more informed decision-making and proactive healthcare management.

Staying Competitive

The competitive landscape in health tech is fierce, with numerous startups and established players vying for market share. M&A activities allow companies to consolidate their positions, eliminate redundancies, and leverage synergies that can lead to cost savings and increased market reach. Additionally, acquiring specialized firms can infuse fresh talent and new technologies into a company’s portfolio, fostering a culture of continuous improvement and innovation.

Real-World Examples of M&A in Health Tech

To illustrate the impact and significance of M&A in health tech, let’s explore two notable real-world examples that have shaped the industry landscape, one highlighting the challenges and the other showcasing successful integration.

Teladoc Health’s Acquisition of Livongo: A Cautionary Tale

In August 2020, Teladoc Health, a leader in telemedicine, announced its acquisition of Livongo, a company specializing in digital chronic condition management, for approximately $18.5 billion. This merger aimed to create a comprehensive virtual care platform by combining Teladoc’s telehealth services with Livongo’s expertise in managing chronic diseases like diabetes and hypertension. Initially, the reaction to the merger was overwhelmingly positive. Investors were excited about the potential of the combined entity, and Teladoc's stock price saw a significant uptick following the announcement.

However, the merger has not been without its challenges. The integration of Teladoc and Livongo revealed substantial hurdles, primarily stemming from differing corporate cultures and operating models. These disparities made it difficult to unify the two organizations seamlessly, leading to increased employee turnover and delays in the rollout of new products and services. The cultural clash not only affected team cohesion but also impeded the efficient implementation of integrated healthcare solutions, ultimately impacting patient care and company performance.

Philips’ acquisition of BioTelemetry

Philips completed its approximately $2.8 billion BioTelemetry acquisition on 9 February 2021. BioTelemetry provided remote cardiac diagnostics and monitoring. Philips described the deal as a way to combine care in hospitals and at home.

The Impact on Recruitment: Bridging Health and Technology Expertise

As M&A activity intensifies in the health tech sector, the demand for talent with a dual expertise in healthcare and technology has surged. Companies are no longer solely seeking professionals with deep technical skills or extensive healthcare knowledge; instead, they are looking for individuals who can bridge both domains effectively.

Hybrid Skill Sets

Recruitment in health tech now emphasizes hybrid skill sets that encompass both clinical understanding and technological proficiency. Professionals who can navigate the complexities of healthcare regulations, patient care protocols, and data privacy, while also being adept at software development, data analytics, and digital innovation, are highly sought after. This blend of skills ensures that technology solutions are not only innovative but also practical and compliant with healthcare standards.

Interdisciplinary Teams

M&A often results in the integration of diverse teams, necessitating collaboration between healthcare practitioners, IT specialists, data scientists, and business strategists. Companies are investing in interdisciplinary teams that can drive the development and implementation of comprehensive health tech solutions. Recruitment strategies are evolving to identify and attract talent that thrives in such collaborative environments and can contribute to cross-functional initiatives.

Continuous Learning and Adaptability

The dynamic nature of health tech requires professionals to be lifelong learners, continuously updating their knowledge and skills to keep pace with technological advancements and evolving healthcare needs. Employers are prioritizing candidates who demonstrate adaptability, a willingness to embrace change, and a commitment to ongoing professional development.

Navigating Challenges in Health Tech M&A

While the benefits of M&A in health tech are substantial, the process is not without challenges. Successful mergers and acquisitions require careful planning, strategic alignment, and effective integration to realize the intended synergies.

Cultural Integration

One of the critical challenges in health tech M&A is the integration of company cultures. Differences in organizational values, work environments, and operational practices can impede collaboration and hinder the achievement of strategic goals. Companies must prioritize cultural alignment and foster an environment of mutual respect and understanding to ensure a smooth transition.

Regulatory Compliance

Healthcare is a highly regulated industry, and mergers or acquisitions must navigate complex compliance requirements. Ensuring that all aspects of the deal adhere to healthcare regulations, data privacy laws, and industry standards is paramount. Failure to address regulatory concerns can result in legal complications and undermine the success of the merger.

Technological Compatibility

Integrating diverse technological systems and platforms poses another significant challenge. Ensuring compatibility, data interoperability, and seamless integration of digital tools is essential for delivering unified and efficient healthcare services. Companies must invest in robust integration strategies and leverage scalable technologies to overcome technical barriers.

The Future of M&A in Health Tech

Looking ahead, the trend of mergers and acquisitions in health tech is expected to continue its upward trajectory. Several factors will shape the future landscape of M&A in this sector:

Advancements in Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

AI and machine learning will play a pivotal role in driving innovation within health tech. Companies that can harness these technologies to develop predictive analytics, personalized treatment plans, and automated diagnostic tools will be prime candidates for strategic acquisitions.

Expansion of Telehealth and Remote Monitoring

The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated the adoption of telehealth and remote patient monitoring solutions. As healthcare providers and patients become more comfortable with virtual care, companies specializing in these areas will likely see increased M&A activity to expand their service offerings and reach.

Focus on Data Security and Privacy

With the increasing reliance on digital technologies comes the heightened importance of data security and privacy. Mergers and acquisitions will increasingly focus on companies that can offer robust cybersecurity solutions and ensure compliance with stringent data protection regulations.

Integration of Genomics and Personalized Medicine

The integration of genomics and personalized medicine into healthcare will drive M&A activity as companies seek to incorporate genetic data analysis and tailored treatment plans into their service offerings. Acquiring firms with expertise in genomics will enable healthcare providers to offer more precise and effective care.

Conclusion

Health technology acquisitions can bring new products and services together. They also bring challenges for people, systems and customers. Hiring and integration plans should address both the opportunity and the work needed to deliver it.
